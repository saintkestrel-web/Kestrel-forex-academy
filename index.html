Lesson 1: Introduction to Forex

What is Forex?

Forex means foreign exchange. It is the market where one currency is exchanged for another. Traders buy or sell currency pairs based on changes in their exchange rates.

Example:
EUR/USD = 1.1000 means one euro is worth 1.10 US dollars.

How does Forex trading work?

If you buy EUR/USD, you are buying euros and selling US dollars. If the price rises, your position can make a profit. If it falls, your position can lose money.

If you sell EUR/USD, you are selling euros and buying US dollars. A falling price can benefit your position.

Important facts

  • Forex is traded in currency pairs.
  • Prices change because of supply, demand and economic events.
  • Leverage can increase both profits and losses.
  • Trading does not guarantee profits.
Lesson 2: Currency Pairs — Major, Minor and Exotic

What is a currency pair?

A currency pair compares the value of one currency with another. The first currency is the base currency, and the second is the quote currency.

Major pairs

Major pairs contain the US dollar and another major currency. They are generally among the most actively traded pairs.

  • EUR/USD — Euro / US dollar
  • GBP/USD — British pound / US dollar
  • USD/JPY — US dollar / Japanese yen
  • USD/CHF — US dollar / Swiss franc
  • AUD/USD — Australian dollar / US dollar
  • USD/CAD — US dollar / Canadian dollar
  • NZD/USD — New Zealand dollar / US dollar

Minor pairs

Minor pairs combine major currencies without including the US dollar.

  • EUR/GBP — Euro / British pound
  • EUR/JPY — Euro / Japanese yen
  • GBP/JPY — British pound / Japanese yen
  • AUD/JPY — Australian dollar / Japanese yen
  • EUR/CHF — Euro / Swiss franc

Exotic pairs

Exotic pairs combine a major currency with a currency from a smaller or emerging market.

  • USD/NGN — US dollar / Nigerian naira
  • USD/TRY — US dollar / Turkish lira
  • USD/ZAR — US dollar / South African rand
  • USD/MXN — US dollar / Mexican peso
Remember:
Exotic pairs often have wider spreads and lower liquidity. The pairs available to you depend on your broker.
Lesson 3: Base Currency and Quote Currency

In a currency pair, the base currency is written first. The quote currency is written second.

EUR/USD = 1.1000

Base currency: EUR
Quote currency: USD
Meaning: 1 EUR = 1.10 USD

If EUR/USD rises from 1.1000 to 1.1100, the euro has strengthened relative to the dollar.

If EUR/USD falls from 1.1000 to 1.0900, the euro has weakened relative to the dollar.

The direction of the pair tells you how the base currency is valued against the quote currency.

Lesson 4: Bid Price and Ask Price

Bid price

The bid is the price at which the market or broker is willing to buy the base currency from you. It is generally the price used to close a standard market-buy position.

Ask price

The ask is the price at which the market or broker is willing to sell the base currency to you. It is generally the price used to open a standard market-buy position.

EUR/USD
Bid: 1.1000
Ask: 1.1002

A market buy normally opens at the ask and closes at the bid. A market sell normally opens at the bid and closes at the ask. This difference contributes to your trading cost.

Lesson 5: Spread and Trading Costs

The spread is the difference between the ask price and the bid price.

Ask = 1.1002
Bid = 1.1000
Spread = 0.0002 = 2 pips on this five-digit-style quote.

A wider spread means a higher initial spread cost for the same position size. Spreads can widen during news, low-liquidity periods and market transitions.

Other trading costs

  • Commission, where charged.
  • Swap or overnight financing, where applicable.
  • Slippage when execution differs from the requested price.

Always check your broker's actual spread and contract specifications before entering a trade.

Lesson 6: Pips, Points and Pipettes

What is a pip?

A pip is a standard unit for measuring price movement in Forex. For most currency pairs, one pip is 0.0001. For many JPY pairs, one pip is 0.01.

EUR/USD moves from 1.1000 to 1.1020.
Movement = 20 pips.
USD/JPY moves from 150.00 to 150.20.
Movement = 20 pips.

What is a pipette?

On a five-digit EUR/USD quote such as 1.10005, the fifth decimal is a fractional pip, often called a pipette. Ten pipettes equal one pip.

What is a point?

The meaning of a point depends on the platform and symbol. On many five-digit Forex quotes, one point is 0.00001, while one pip is 0.0001. Always check the symbol's digits.

Gold and other CFDs can use different quoting conventions, so do not assume every instrument follows the same pip rule.

Lesson 7: Lot Sizes and Position Volume

A lot measures the size of your Forex position. For many standard Forex contracts, the common lot sizes are:

  • Standard lot: 100,000 units of the base currency.
  • Mini lot: 10,000 units.
  • Micro lot: 1,000 units.
  • Nano lot: 100 units, where supported.
1.00 lot = 100,000 units
0.10 lot = 10,000 units
0.01 lot = 1,000 units

How lot size affects risk

For EUR/USD, a standard lot often has a pip value of approximately $10 when the account currency is USD. A 0.10 lot position would then be approximately $1 per pip, and a 0.01 lot position approximately $0.10 per pip.

If 0.01 lot moves against you by 20 pips, the loss would be approximately $2 before other costs, under the example above.

Pip value varies by pair, exchange rate, contract size and account currency. Gold and other CFDs can use different contract specifications.

Lesson 8: Leverage

Leverage allows you to control a position larger than the money required as margin.

With 1:100 leverage, a position worth $10,000 may require approximately $100 of margin, before adjustments and broker-specific requirements.

Leverage does not make a trade safer. Your profit and loss depend mainly on position size and price movement.

  • Higher leverage can reduce the margin required.
  • Large positions can produce large losses quickly.
  • Broker leverage limits can change around certain events.

Choose your position size based on your acceptable loss, not on the maximum leverage your broker offers.

Lesson 9: Margin and Free Margin

Margin is the amount of account funds reserved to support an open position. It is not the same as the amount you intend to risk on your stop loss.

  • Used margin: funds reserved for open positions.
  • Free margin: equity minus used margin.
  • Equity: balance plus or minus floating profit or loss.
  • Margin level: equity divided by used margin, multiplied by 100%.
Equity = $100
Used margin = $20
Free margin = $80
Margin level = 500%

If losses reduce equity, your margin level can fall. Brokers may issue margin calls or close positions according to their specific margin rules.

Lesson 10: Balance, Equity and Floating Profit

Balance

Balance is your account's result after completed trades, deposits, withdrawals and applicable charges. It does not normally include the unrealized result of open trades.

Equity

Equity reflects your balance plus or minus the current floating results of open positions, adjusted for applicable account charges.

Balance = $100
Floating profit = $5
Equity = $105, assuming no other adjustments.

If the floating result changes to a $5 loss, equity becomes $95 under the same assumptions.

Remember: an open trade's profit can change before the position is closed.

Lesson 11: Buy Orders and Sell Orders

Buy

A buy position generally benefits when the instrument's price rises after your entry.

Sell

A sell position generally benefits when the instrument's price falls after your entry.

Common order types

  • Market order: requests execution at the available market price.
  • Buy Limit: a pending buy below the current price.
  • Sell Limit: a pending sell above the current price.
  • Buy Stop: a pending buy above the current price.
  • Sell Stop: a pending sell below the current price.

Pending-order placement and execution can depend on the broker's rules and market conditions.

Lesson 12: Calculating Profit and Loss

Your trading result depends on the price movement, position size, instrument specifications and trading costs.

Example: EUR/USD
Position: 0.10 lot
Approximate pip value: $1
Favourable movement: 30 pips
Approximate gross profit: $30.

If the same position moves 30 pips against you, the approximate gross loss would be $30.

These figures are illustrative. Commission, spread, swap, slippage and changing conversion rates can affect the final result.

Always confirm the tick size, contract size and tick value for the exact instrument you are trading.

Lesson 13: Stop Loss and Take Profit

Stop Loss (SL)

A stop loss is an order intended to close a position if price reaches a specified level, helping limit losses. Execution can differ from the requested price during gaps or fast markets.

Take Profit (TP)

A take profit is an order intended to close a position when price reaches your target.

Buy entry: 1.1000
Stop loss: 1.0980
Take profit: 1.1060

Potential price risk: 20 pips
Potential price reward: 60 pips

The example has a 1:3 risk-to-reward ratio before costs. A stop loss does not guarantee the exact exit price.

Lesson 14: Risk-to-Reward Ratio

Risk-to-reward compares the amount you are prepared to lose with the potential profit you are targeting.

1:2 means risking $10 to target $20.
1:3 means risking $10 to target $30.

How to calculate it

Divide the potential reward by the potential risk. If the stop-loss distance is 20 pips and the target distance is 60 pips, the ratio is 1:3.

Risk management rules

  • Decide your acceptable loss before entering.
  • Calculate position size from your stop-loss distance.
  • Include costs when evaluating a setup.
  • Do not widen a stop loss simply to avoid taking a loss.

A 1:3 setup can still lose. A favourable ratio does not guarantee profitability; your strategy must also perform well over a meaningful number of trades.

Lesson 15: Asian, London and New York Sessions

Asian session

Trading activity often centres on Asian currencies. Some pairs move within narrower ranges, although strong moves can still happen.

London session

London is a major Forex trading centre. Activity and volatility can increase when European markets become active.

New York session

New York activity can affect the US dollar and other currencies. The overlap with London can be particularly active.

Traders often monitor session highs and lows for potential liquidity, breakouts and reversals. These levels are references, not guaranteed trading signals.

Session behaviour varies by pair, weekday, economic news and market conditions.

Lesson 16: Forex Market Hours and Trading Conditions

The global Forex market generally operates 24 hours a day during the working week, across different financial centres. Retail trading hours depend on your broker and instrument.

What affects market hours?

  • Daylight-saving changes in different countries.
  • Broker server time and scheduled maintenance.
  • Public holidays and reduced liquidity.
  • Weekend closures and market gaps.

Why do prices move?

Prices respond to buying and selling activity, interest-rate expectations, inflation data, employment reports, central-bank decisions and other economic or geopolitical developments.

Before trading

  • Check your broker's current trading schedule.
  • Check the economic calendar for important releases.
  • Be aware of spread widening and slippage.
  • Avoid trading simply because the market is open.

A disciplined trader waits for a suitable setup instead of forcing a trade every day.
